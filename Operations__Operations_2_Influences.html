🌐 Operations 2 · Influences on Operations

Operations · ① Role · ② Influences ← you are here · ③ Processes · ④ Strategies · 📕 Full note

Scope: the full HSC Operations module is examinable. This file covers the Influences on operations management dot points only.

1. The Seven Influences

Influence Detail
Globalisation Removal of trade barriers; operations structured around global production facilities. Opportunity: global markets, economies of scale, global sourcing. Threat: intensified competition
Technology Increased efficiency, reduced costs, consistent quality, higher productivity. Includes both administrative and processing technology
Quality expectations Goods: design, fitness for purpose, durability. Services: professionalism, reliability, level of customisation. Failure → dissatisfaction, returns, damaged reputation
Cost-based competition Pressure to lower production costs. Methods: eliminate waste, standardise, high volume, automate, economies of scale, bulk buying
Government policies Changes in taxation, industrial relations, environmental regulation, training requirements — all create compliance costs
Legal regulation Compliance with legislation across WHS, environment, food safety, fair trading and industrial relations. Compliance costs are the expenses of meeting these requirements — see §2 below
Environmental sustainability Operating without compromising resources for future generations. Methods: minimise waste, recycle, reduce carbon footprint

2. Legal Regulation — The Actual Acts

Naming the Act with its year and jurisdiction is a mark. "The Environmental Protection Act" is not an answer — no NSW Act has that name. (Cth) = Commonwealth, national. (NSW) = state.

Act What the Act actually requires What it costs operations Regulator
Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW) Imposes a duty of care — the employer must eliminate or minimise risks so far as is reasonably practicable, identify hazards, train and supervise staff, and consult workers on safety decisions Safety equipment, training time, signage, incident reporting systems, safety audits SafeWork NSW
Workers Compensation Act 1987 (NSW) Every employer must hold a workers compensation insurance policy. Injured workers receive income support and medical costs on a no-fault basis — they don't have to prove the employer was negligent Insurance premiums, which rise with the business's claims history icare · SIRA
Protection of the Environment Operations Act 1997 (NSW) (the POEO Act) Businesses conducting scheduled activities (chemical production, waste processing, electricity generation, intensive livestock) must hold an environment protection licence. Empowers the EPA to issue clean-up, prevention and prohibition notices. Penalties to $5 million for corporations Licence fees, emissions monitoring, effluent treatment, waste disposal contracts NSW EPA
Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (Cth) (the EPBC Act) Any action likely to have a significant impact on a matter of national environmental significance — threatened species, world heritage areas, wetlands — must be referred for federal approval Environmental impact assessment, project delays, redesign Federal environment department
Waste Avoidance and Resource Recovery Act 2001 (NSW) Sets NSW waste reduction targets and imposes a waste levy on material sent to landfill, deliberately making disposal more expensive than recovery The levy itself, plus separation and recycling systems NSW EPA
Food Act 2003 (NSW) Businesses handling food must notify the authority, appoint a food safety supervisor, and keep premises, equipment and handling practices to the Food Standards Code. Creates offences for selling unsafe or falsely described food Supervisor training, temperature monitoring, cleaning schedules, inspections NSW Food Authority
Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth) (contains the Australian Consumer Law, Schedule 2) Bans misleading and deceptive conduct, guarantees goods are of acceptable quality and fit for purpose, and imposes product safety standards and mandatory recalls. Also bans anti-competitive conduct such as price fixing and resale price maintenance Quality assurance systems, accurate labelling, recall capability, warranty servicing ACCC
Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) Establishes the National Employment Standards (11 minimum entitlements), minimum wages, awards, enterprise bargaining, and protection from unfair dismissal. Includes the right to rest and meal breaks under the relevant award Wage costs, rostering constraints, penalty rates, redundancy entitlements Fair Work Commission · Fair Work Ombudsman
Modern Slavery Act 2018 (Cth) Businesses with annual revenue over $100 million must publish an annual modern slavery statement describing the risks of forced labour in their operations and supply chains, and what they are doing about them Supplier auditing, due diligence, annual reporting Anti-Slavery Commissioner
Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 and 1997 (Cth) · A New Tax System (GST) Act 1999 (Cth) Company tax on assessable income, and 10% GST collected on taxable supplies and remitted to the ATO Record keeping, BAS lodgement, accounting systems ATO

⚠️ The Trade Practices Act 1974 no longer exists. It was renamed the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth). It appears as an MCQ distractor and is never correct for anything current.

2.1 How to use an Act in an answer

Naming it is one mark. The mark after it comes from the consequence.

1. NAME    Under the [ACT YEAR (JURISDICTION)],
2. DUTY    the business [OBLIGATION].
3. BREACH  [STIMULUS FACT] constitutes a breach,
4. COST    exposing it to [PENALTY / CLAIM / REPUTATIONAL DAMAGE].
5. ACTION  The business should therefore [COMPLIANCE MEASURE].

Worked:

Under the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW), the business owes a duty of care to provide a safe environment for employees and customers. Floors that are often wet and slippery constitute a direct breach, exposing it to workers compensation claims, public liability claims and regulatory penalties. Operations should therefore introduce scheduled cleaning, hazard signage and staff safety training, accepting the compliance cost as substantially cheaper than a single serious injury claim.

The two phrases that pay in any legal question: compliance costs (the expense of meeting the law) and duty of care (the WHS obligation). Both appear in marking criteria constantly.

2.2 Which Act for which scenario

Scenario Act
A worker slips on a wet floor Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW)
That worker claims medical costs and lost income Workers Compensation Act 1987 (NSW)
A factory discharges effluent into a creek Protection of the Environment Operations Act 1997 (NSW)
A restaurant serves food causing illness Food Act 2003 (NSW)
A product injures a customer through a manufacturing fault Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth)
Staff are made to work through their breaks Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)
A business cannot verify its overseas supplier's labour conditions Modern Slavery Act 2018 (Cth)

3. Corporate Social Responsibility

CSR = open and accountable business actions based on respect for people, community, society and the environment.

The triple bottom line = financial + social + environmental performance.

The cost of CSR: Environmentally sustainable practices often cost more in the short term (new equipment, sustainable inputs, waste treatment), which conflicts with cost leadership. The counter-argument is long-term reputation, customer loyalty and avoided regulatory penalties.

⚠️ Confusing pairs in this section

Pair The tell
Legal compliance vs ethical responsibility Compliance = the letter of the law, the minimum required. Ethics = the spirit of the law. A business can be fully compliant and still be criticised as unethical
Compliance cost vs penalty Compliance cost is the expense of meeting the law. A penalty is what you pay for breaching it
Government policies vs legal regulation Policies are what the government chooses to do (tax rates, training programs). Regulation is what the law requires
Triple bottom line vs being green Triple bottom line is three measures — financial, social, environmental. Not just environmental

📖 Definitions to quote

Term Definition
Compliance cost The expense incurred by a business in meeting legal requirements
Duty of care The WHS obligation on an employer to eliminate or minimise risks so far as is reasonably practicable
CSR Open and accountable business actions based on respect for people, community, society and the environment
Triple bottom line Measuring performance across financial, social and environmental dimensions
Environmental sustainability Operating without compromising the resources of future generations
