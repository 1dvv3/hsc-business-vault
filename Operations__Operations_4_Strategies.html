🎯 Operations 4 · Operations Strategies

Operations · ① Role · ② Influences · ③ Processes · ④ Strategies ← you are here · 📕 Full note

Scope: the full HSC Operations module is examinable. This file covers the Operations strategies dot points only.

⭐ The highest-yield section in the module. Most 20-mark Operations questions are answered mainly out of this file.

1. Performance Objectives

Objective Definition
Quality Design quality (how well it matches consumer understanding of what it should do) + conformance quality (how well it meets specifications) + quality of service
Speed Fast response to market changes; reduced lead times and waiting times
Dependability Consistency and reliability — the product works as promised, and delivery arrives when promised
Flexibility The ability to adjust production to change quickly — in volume, product or delivery
Customisation Creating individualised products to meet specific customer needs; mass customisation delivers this at volume
Cost Minimising expenses so the business can compete on price while remaining profitable

2. New Product or Service Design and Development

Two approaches:

Approach Definition
Consumer-driven Customer preferences and market research determine the design
Technology-driven Innovation and new technology enable products consumers had not asked for

Steps: Market research → Design and prototype → Testing → Refinement → Production and launch

Key considerations: supply chain (do we need new suppliers?), quality (can standards be maintained?), capacity (do we need new resources or technology?), cost.

Service design:

  • Explicit service — the tangible parts: the time taken, the expertise applied
  • Implicit service — the intangible parts: the feeling of wellbeing, status, comfort
  • Cost is determined after both elements are specified

3. Supply Chain Management

SCM = the integration and management of the flow of supplies from inputs, through transformation, to outputs.

Sourcing = deciding which suppliers to use. Factors: consumer demand, quality of inputs, flexibility and timeliness, cost.

Trends in sourcing:

  • Supplier rationalisation — reducing the number of suppliers to gain better terms and consistency
  • Backwards vertical integration — acquiring your own supplier
  • Cost minimisation — moving sourcing offshore
  • Building flexible and responsive supply chains

Global sourcing = purchasing inputs without being constrained by location.

Advantages Disadvantages
Lower costs Quality risk
Reduced lead times (if well managed) Impact on domestic businesses and jobs
Access to materials unavailable domestically Communication, cultural and time-zone barriers
Access to specialist expertise Longer, more fragile supply lines

E-commerce = using the internet in supply chain management. E-procurement systems automate ordering. B2B (business to business — automatic restocking between firms) and B2C (business to consumer — direct sales).

Logistics = distribution, transportation, storage (warehousing and distribution centres), materials handling and packaging.

4. Outsourcing

Outsourcing = the use of external providers to perform business activities — either domestic or global.

Advantages Disadvantages
Cost savings Communication and language barriers
Improved efficiency Loss of direct quality control
Access to specialist skills Security and confidentiality risks
Focus on core business Dependency on the partner
Increased accountability through contracts Loss of corporate memory and in-house skill
Simplification of the business Reputation affected by the partner's conduct

5. Technology

Type Detail
Leading edge Newest available; competitive advantage; expensive and untested
Established Proven and widely used; reliable and cheaper; no differentiation

Key applications: CAD (computer-aided design), CAM (computer-aided manufacture), robotics, automation.

6. Inventory Management

⚠️ Critical exam distinction If the question asks about inventory MANAGEMENT → answer Holding stock (JIC) or Just in Time (JIT). If the question asks about a strategy to VALUE inventory, or an inventory VALUATION method → answer FIFO or LIFO. Mixing these up is one of the most common ways students lose marks in this topic.

6.1 Holding Stock — "Just in Case" (JIC)

Advantages Disadvantages
Can always meet customer demand Holding costs: storage, insurance, theft, spoilage, security
Alternatives available if an item is out of stock Capital tied up in stock that could be used elsewhere
Reduced lead times for customers Risk of obsolescence (stock becoming outdated)
Immediate revenue from available stock Damage and deterioration
Bulk-buying cost savings Requires warehouse space

6.2 Just in Time (JIT)

Produce or order only what is needed, when it is needed.

Advantages Disadvantages
Greatly reduced storage costs Lead time increases when demand varies unexpectedly
No theft or damage risk from stored stock Total dependence on supplier reliability
No obsolescence A single delivery failure can shut down production
Frees working capital Loses bulk-buying discounts

6.3 Inventory Valuation — FIFO, LIFO and Weighted Average

Method Definition
FIFO (First In First Out) Assumes the oldest stock is sold first
LIFO (Last In First Out) Assumes the newest stock is sold first
Weighted average Uses the average cost of all inventory; smooths price fluctuations

The decision table — memorise this:

Understate / minimise profit (to reduce tax) Overstate / maximise profit (for potential investors)
Cost of inventory is increasing LIFO FIFO
Cost of inventory is decreasing FIFO LIFO

Worked example — FIFO vs LIFO with rising costs

Purchases:

Date Units Cost per unit Total
1 Jan 100 $10 $1,000
1 Feb 100 $12 $1,200
1 Mar 100 $14 $1,400
Total 300 $3,600

150 units are sold at $20 each → Sales revenue = $3,000

FIFO (oldest sold first): COGS = (100 × $10) + (50 × $12) = 1,000 + 600 = $1,600 Closing stock = (50 × $12) + (100 × $14) = 600 + 1,400 = $2,000 Gross profit = 3,000 − 1,600 = $1,400

LIFO (newest sold first): COGS = (100 × $14) + (50 × $12) = 1,400 + 600 = $2,000 Closing stock = (50 × $12) + (100 × $10) = 600 + 1,000 = $1,600 Gross profit = 3,000 − 2,000 = $1,000

Weighted average: $3,600 ÷ 300 units = $12 per unit COGS = 150 × 12 = $1,800 → Gross profit = $1,200

Conclusion: With rising costs, FIFO reports the higher profit ($1,400) — chosen to impress investors. LIFO reports the lower profit ($1,000) — chosen to reduce tax. Weighted average sits between the two. Note COGS + closing stock = $3,600 in every method: the total cost is the same, only its allocation differs.

EOQ (Economic Order Quantity): the optimal order quantity that minimises total inventory costs — balancing ordering costs (which fall as you order less often in bigger batches) against holding costs (which rise as you store more).

LIFO note: LIFO is permitted for HSC analysis but is not permitted under Australian Accounting Standards for external financial reporting. Answer the question as asked, using the table above.

7. Quality Management

Approach Key feature
Quality control Inspection at various points in production. Define standards, test against them, take corrective action. Detects defects after they occur
Quality assurance A system designed to prevent defects — "right the first time." Certified against external standards such as ISO 9000
Total Quality Management (TQM) Holistic — every employee is responsible for quality; the total business is managed for quality. Elements: customer focus, continuous improvement, employee empowerment
Six Sigma Data-driven; targets 3.4 defects per million opportunities. Uses DMAIC: Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control
Kaizen Continuous incremental improvement involving all employees; eliminating waste (muda)
Quality circles Small groups of employees who meet regularly to identify and solve quality problems

⭐ The three-way tell — this was 2025 HSC Q8 and you lost it.

If the stem says… The answer is
Inspecting, checking, testing, detecting faults Control
A system, a standard, certification, preventing defects, "right the first time", ISO 9000 Assurance
"Ongoing commitment", continuous, never-ending, always getting better, Kaizen Improvement

2025 HSC Q8 read "demonstrates an ongoing commitment to make its operations more efficient and effective" → Improvement. You answered Assurance. Assurance is something you install once and certify; improvement is something you keep doing.

8. Overcoming Resistance to Change

Two sources of resistance:

Source Detail
Financial costs Purchasing new equipment, redundancy payouts, retraining costs, reorganising plant layout
Psychological (inertia) Fear and uncertainty → less motivated staff → lower productivity → quality issues → increased lead times and defects → lost customers

Inertia = the tendency of people and organisations to keep doing what they have always done and resist any change to it.

Managing change effectively:

  1. Identify the sources of change and assess the need for it
  2. Communicate with employees and build widespread support
  3. Use change agents — people who champion and drive the change
  4. Apply change models (e.g. Lewin's unfreeze → change → refreeze)

9. Global Factors

Factor Detail
Global sourcing Sourcing inputs anywhere in the world for cost or quality advantage
Economies of scale Higher volumes → lower per-unit costs, through capital investment, technology, global branding and centralised training
Scanning and learning Actively studying and adopting global best practice from other markets and competitors
Research and development Investing in leading-edge technologies; global expertise increases the opportunity base

⚠️ Confusing pairs in this section

Pair The tell
Speed vs dependability Speed = how fast. Dependability = how reliably you deliver when you said you would. You can be fast and undependable
Quality control vs quality assurance Control inspects to find defects (reactive). Assurance designs the process so they don't happen (proactive)
JIT/JIC vs FIFO/LIFO JIT/JIC = inventory management. FIFO/LIFO = inventory valuation
Outsourcing vs offshoring Outsourcing = an external provider. Offshoring = overseas, which may still be in-house
Global sourcing vs outsourcing Global sourcing buys inputs. Outsourcing contracts out an activity
Leading edge vs established technology Leading edge = advantage but untested and dear. Established = reliable and cheap but no advantage
Economies of scale Lower per-unit cost. Total cost still rises with volume
Design quality vs conformance quality Design = matches what consumers think it should do. Conformance = meets the specification
Explicit vs implicit service Explicit = tangible (time, expertise). Implicit = intangible (comfort, status)
Obsolescence vs spoilage Obsolescence = outdated. Spoilage = physically deteriorated
EOQ vs reorder point EOQ = how much to order. Reorder point = when to order

📝 Exam habits

Every extended response ends at cost leadership or differentiation. That is the strategic role, and markers look for the link.

Don't list influences or strategies — explain the impact on the operations process. A list is a Band 3 answer.
