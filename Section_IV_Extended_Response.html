📝 Section IV — The Extended Response

20 marks · about 35 minutes · choose ONE of two questions · separate writing booklet

No stimulus. You bring the case study. The two questions cover different topics, so pick the one where your case study evidence is strongest. Scaffolds below follow your teacher's Section IV Essay Structures handout. Case study data: Case Studies · Theory: Operations | Marketing | Finance | Human Resources

1. What You Are Marked On

Criterion What it actually means
Demonstrate knowledge and understanding relevant to the question Correct syllabus content, defined
Apply relevant business case study/studies and contemporary business issues Real businesses. Real figures. Real dates. This is where most marks are lost
Communicate using relevant business terminology and concepts Syllabus terms, not everyday words
Present a sustained, logical and cohesive response Judgement in the introduction, sustained through every paragraph, restated at the end

No headings here. Section III is a report. Section IV is an essay — continuous prose, paragraphed.

2. Identify the Verb First

The verb dictates the entire structure. Get it wrong and the content can't save you.

Verb What to do Structure
Assess / Evaluate / Explain the importance of Make a judgement §3 scaffold
Analyse / Explain how Show relationships and draw out implications §4 scaffold
Discuss For and against, then judge §3 scaffold, balanced
Examine Identify components and their relationships §4 scaffold

3. Assess · Evaluate · Explain the importance of

Judgement based on criteria — does it help the business achieve ________?

Introduction

1. Start with the JUDGEMENT SENTENCE.
2. State specifically what you are evaluating — use the syllabus dot points by name.
3. Name every case study you will use.

Your teacher's model:

"This essay will evaluate how Apple effectively uses their operational strategies of Supply Chain Management, Inventory Management and Outsourcing to respond to the globalisation, ethical and legal influences."

Notice: the dot points are named, not gestured at.

Each body paragraph

1  JUDGEMENT   Answer the question — a judgement with a brief overview of reasons.
2  DEFINE      Define and describe the key concept(s).
3  ADVANTAGES  Prove they ARE advantages by linking to a topic-specific goal.
                 Use statistics as evidence.
4  DISADVANT.  Prove they ARE disadvantages by linking to a topic-specific goal.
5  RESTATE     Re-state the judgement, linked to long-term business goals.

"Prove they are advantages by linking to a topic-specific goal." An advantage isn't an advantage because you say so. It's an advantage because it helps achieve cost leadership, or market share, or liquidity, or staff retention. Name the goal.

Conclusion

One to two summary sentences per body paragraph, each including the case study.

4. Analyse · Explain how · Examine

Show the relationship between things and draw out implications.

Introduction

1. Start by LINKING THE TWO IDEAS TOGETHER — cause and effect in the first sentence.
2. State specifically what you are analysing — name the syllabus dot points.
3. Name every case study.

Your teacher's model:

"This essay will analyse the impacts for Apple of globalisation, ethical and legal influences on their operational strategies of Supply Chain Management, Inventory Management and Outsourcing."

Each body paragraph

1  LINK        Open by linking the two ideas — cause and effect — for the case study.
2  CAUSE/EFFECT 1   HOW is the business impacted? (observable changes)
                    WHY is it affected? (link to a topic-specific goal)
3  CAUSE/EFFECT 2   HOW · WHY · and link back to cause #1.
4  IMPLICATIONS     LONG TERM. Link to the strategic role and business goals.
                    Positive, negative, or both — but detailed and tied to the case study.

You do not have to give both positive and negative implications. You do have to make them detailed and tied to the case study.

5. Worked Body Paragraph — 2021 HSC Marketing

Question: Analyse how a situational analysis (SWOT, product life cycle) may affect marketing strategies.

Teacher's structural rule for this question:

To score above 12, you must have at least one paragraph on SWOT and one on Product Life Cycle. SWOT — 2 body paragraphs. Each takes ONE element and does at least two of it (2 strengths, or 2 threats, etc.). Product Life Cycle — 1 body paragraph showing how ONE marketing strategy changes across the cycle.

The paragraph, annotated:

Apple has numerous threats such as competition in its smartphone market which impacts on their marketing strategy such as their Product strategy. [LINK — cause and effect up front] Threats are external factors that could impact on the business that they have no control over [DEFINITION] and for Apple one of their major threats is the increased competition especially from low cost Asian providers such as Huawei, Xiaomi. The product strategy includes their branding, packaging and after sales services [DEFINITION]. For Apple to compete against low cost competitors they have positioned their product as a high quality, luxury smartphone. Apple's branding is easily recognizable with the apple with a bite out of it and they have linked the branding to their reputation of producing a high quality, innovative product [HOW]. However there has been some issues with whether they have been innovative over the last few years as their focus has been on improving the camera. This has impacted on their market share as it was lower than Samsung from Quarter 2 2023 (17% compared to 20%) until Quarter 4, 2024 when their market share improved to be higher than Samsung (23% compared to 16%) [EFFECT — with statistics]. … This meets the needs of the customers expectations when purchasing Apple products therefore their target market is more likely to purchase their products rather than their cheaper competitors [WHY/EFFECT]. Therefore by using their product strategy of branding and packaging Apple is able to maintain or increase the number of their customers and have been able to minimise the threat of competitors and improve their market share in the last quarter of 2024. [IMPLICATION]

What makes it work:

Move Where
Cause and effect in sentence 1 "threats… which impacts on their marketing strategy such as their Product strategy"
Both concepts defined threats, then product strategy
Named competitors Huawei, Xiaomi, Samsung
Dated statistics 17% vs 20% Q2 2023 → 23% vs 16% Q4 2024
Honest weakness "issues with whether they have been innovative"
Implication closes market share improved, threat minimised

The dated statistic is the difference between Band 5 and Band 6. "Apple has a large market share" is worth nothing. "23% compared to Samsung's 16% in Q4 2024" is worth a mark.

6. Short-Answer Verb Scaffolds

Same logic, compressed. From your teacher's 2024 Operations Past Paper short answer structure.

Assess — "Assess the impact of customised products on the operations process of the sports shoe store."

1  Judgement sentence: "It has a large impact on the operational process of…"
2  Define the process
3  How is it impacting the operational process
4  Effect on the business
5  What it means for the business (implication)
6  Restate the judgement

Analyse — "Analyse the use of outsourcing in achieving the strategic role of Operations Management."

1  Define outsourcing
2  How does outsourcing impact operations
3  Effect — link to the performance objective
4  What it means for the business (implication) — linking to cost leadership

7. The Marker's Comments — What Full Marks Looks Like

From your Past HSC Finance Model Answer 2025 pack. These are the actual reasons marks were awarded.

A 2-mark answer scored 2/2 because the student:

  • showed all the working for the calculation
  • stated what it means for the business
  • compared it to the industry average
  • related it to the scenario

A 4-mark answer scored 4/4 because the student:

  • linked it to the scenario
  • showed clear cause and effect relationships using relational verbs
  • provided implications, even if limited

Marker's note on that response: "This is probably not the best 4 that a student will write but it does enough to reach the 4 in the HSC." You do not need brilliance. You need the four moves, every time.

Relational verbs are the ones that carry cause and effect:

causes · results in · leads to · means that · therefore · consequently
impacts on · which in turn · thereby · so that · enabling

9. Timing and Checklist

Phase Time
Choose the question, identify the verb, plan 3 paragraphs 5 min
Introduction 4 min
Body ×3 7 min each
Conclusion 3 min

800–900 words, three body paragraphs.

□ Verb identified — judgement scaffold or relationship scaffold
□ Introduction states the judgement or the link, in sentence 1
□ Syllabus dot points named specifically in the introduction
□ Case studies named in the introduction
□ Every paragraph contains the case study
□ At least one DATED STATISTIC per paragraph
□ Every advantage/disadvantage linked to a topic-specific goal
□ Implications are LONG TERM and tied to the strategic role
□ Conclusion: 1–2 sentences per body paragraph, each naming the case study

The three most common ways to lose marks here:

  1. Theory with no business. Every paragraph needs the case study, not just paragraph one.
  2. Undated, unsourced figures. "A large market share" earns nothing.
  3. Answering the wrong verb. Describing when asked to evaluate caps you at mid-band regardless of content.
