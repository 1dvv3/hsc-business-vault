Marketing - Syllabus Summary

1. Role of Marketing

  • Strategic role: translate business objectives, including profit maximisation, into customer-focused plans and sales activity
  • Marketing plan: research-based objectives, target markets and strategies aligned with business goals
  • Customer orientation: understand and satisfy customer needs; continue the relationship after the sale
  • Relationship marketing: loyalty, repeat sales, customer care, rewards and after-sales service
  • Marketing approaches: production, sales, marketing and societal
  • Market types: resource, industrial, intermediate and consumer markets
  • Consumer markets: mass market, market segment and niche market
  • Interdependence:
    • Operations uses demand forecasts and customer feedback to plan production
    • Finance funds marketing and receives revenue generated by sales
    • HR recruits and trains marketing and customer-facing staff

2. Influences on Marketing

  • Customer choice:
    • Psychological: perception, motives, attitudes, personality, self-image and learning
    • Sociocultural: social class, culture, family, roles and reference groups
    • Economic: boom, recession, income, employment and consumer confidence
    • Government: fiscal policy, monetary policy, regulation and industry incentives
  • Ethical influences:
    • Concealed facts, puffery, vague claims, privacy concerns and SUGGING
    • Good taste, health-damaging products, fair competition and pressure selling
  • Legal influences:
    • Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth), including the Australian Consumer Law
    • ACCC and ASIC enforcement
    • Misleading or deceptive conduct, false claims, fine print, bait-and-switch and misleading packaging
    • Consumer guarantees, implied conditions, warranties and refunds for faulty or misdescribed goods
    • Price discrimination restrictions where competition is substantially reduced

3. Marketing Processes

  • Marketing plan sequence: situational analysis → market research → objectives → identify target markets → develop strategies → implementation, monitoring and control
  • Situational analysis:
    • SWOT: internal strengths and weaknesses; external opportunities and threats
    • Product life cycle: introduction, growth, maturity and decline; adapt the mix at each stage
  • Market research:
    • Identify information needs → collect primary/secondary data → analyse and interpret
    • Primary sources: surveys, observation and focus groups; specific and current, but costly and time-consuming
    • Secondary sources: Census, industry reports and existing research; accessible and lower-cost, but may be outdated or broad
  • Marketing objectives: measurable goals aligned with the business, including market share, product mix and customer service
  • Target markets: primary and secondary groups; identify customers to guide product, price, promotion and distribution choices
  • Implementation: put strategies into action, including how, where and when
  • Monitoring and control: compare actual and planned results; use corrective action
  • KPIs: sales analysis, market-share analysis and marketing profitability analysis
  • Strategy revision: modify the 4Ps, develop new products or delete underperforming products

4. Marketing Strategies

  • Market segmentation: geographic, demographic, psychographic and behavioural bases
  • Differentiation: distinguish the offer from competitors; align features and benefits with customer priorities
  • Positioning: desired place/image in the target market; reinforce it across the marketing mix
  • Product:
    • Quality, design, features, packaging, labelling, brand and guarantees
    • Tangible and intangible product elements; adapt product strategy to the life-cycle stage
  • Price:
    • Methods: cost-plus, market-based and competitive pricing
    • Strategies: skimming, penetration, competitive, loss leader, price points, prestige, bundling and dynamic pricing
    • Legal limit: price discrimination must not substantially reduce competition
  • Promotion:
    • Advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, publicity, public relations and direct marketing
    • Match message and channel to target market; distinguish free publicity from planned PR
  • Place/distribution:
    • Direct, one-level, two-level and three-level channels
    • Intensive, selective and exclusive distribution
    • Online retailing, direct-to-consumer sales, warehousing, transport and order processing
  • Extended marketing mix for services:
    • People: staff skills, service and customer interactions
    • Processes: service-delivery steps and convenience
    • Physical evidence: service environment and tangible brand cues
  • E-marketing: web pages, podcasts, SMS, blogs and social media; digital channels affect product information, price comparison, distribution and promotion
  • Global marketing:
    • Global branding and positioning
    • Standardisation, customisation or a combination
    • Global pricing: standardised, customised and market-customised pricing
    • Adjust for exchange rates, transport, taxes, tariffs, language, culture, religion and local tastes
    • Choose promotional channels suited to each market
