👥 Human Resources 1 · Role of Human Resource Management

Human Resources · ① Role ← you are here · ② Influences · ③ Processes · ④ Strategies · 📕 Full note

✅ HSC SCOPE — everything is examinable. The trial (5 Aug 2026) excluded HR Strategies and Effectiveness Indicators. That exclusion has expired. The HSC on Monday 26 October covers the full module.

Definition: Human resource management is the management of people to achieve business objectives. It is one of the four key business functions.

1. Strategic Role of Human Resources

  • HRM aligns the workforce with the strategic direction of the business
  • Attracts, develops and retains talented employees
  • Creates a positive corporate culture
  • Manages employee relations and legal compliance
  • Employees are treated as an asset to be developed, not a cost to be minimised

⭐ Link to performance: Skilled, motivated employees → higher productivity → better operations, more effective marketing, and lower costs to finance. This is the argument to make in every extended response.

2. Interdependence with Other Key Business Functions

Function Relationship with HR
Operations HR recruits and trains production staff. Operations determines skill needs and staffing levels. Productivity depends on workforce quality
Marketing HR recruits the marketing team. Marketing effectiveness depends on staff creativity and skills. Customer-facing staff need service training
Finance Wages and training are major budget items. Finance sets salary budgets; HR must justify expenditure through productivity gains

Exam tip: Always show how HR connects to the other three functions — interdependence is a guaranteed exam concept.

3. Outsourcing HR Functions

Businesses increasingly outsource HR activities to external providers.

Type Detail
Human resource functions Recruitment, payroll, training, and specialist advice contracted to external firms
Using contractors Engaging workers who are not employees. Provides flexibility and avoids on-costs
Domestic contractors Located within Australia
Global contractors Located overseas — lower cost, but risks in quality, communication, time zones and legal jurisdiction

⚠️ Stakeholders are not part of the Role section. The syllabus lists "stakeholders — employers, employees, employer associations, unions, government organisations, society" as the first dot point under HR key influences → Human Resources 2 Influences §1.

⚠️ Confusing pairs in this section

Pair The tell
Employee vs contractor An employee works under a contract of service — the business controls how, when and where, and owes leave, super and award conditions. A contractor works under a contract for service — they control their own methods, invoice for a result, and handle their own tax and insurance. Misclassifying an employee as a contractor is sham contracting and is illegal
Unions vs employer associations Unions represent employees. Employer associations represent employers. Both are stakeholders, opposite sides
Outsourcing vs using contractors Outsourcing hands an entire function to an external firm. Using contractors engages individual workers who are not employees

📖 Definitions to quote

Term Definition
Human resource management The management of people to achieve business objectives
Contractor A worker engaged under a contract for service
Sham contracting Illegally treating an employee as if they were a contractor
Corporate culture The shared values, attitudes and behaviours of the organisation
Outsourcing (HR) Contracting HR functions to external providers
