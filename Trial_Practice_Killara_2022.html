The four missed

Q Chose Correct The lesson
4 C Industrial B Resource Farmers operate in the resource market, where raw materials are sold. The industrial market is who they sell to. The question asked which market they sell in
6 D Performance appraisal C Unfair dismissal Performance appraisal is an evaluation process, not a termination. Justin was sacked for reporting a colleague — harsh, unjust and unreasonable
10 B Enterprise Agreement C Common Law Contract Enterprise agreements are collective and must pass the BOOT. One solicitor negotiating a bespoke $300k package individually is a common law contract
17 C Employees B Finance The stem says "which key business function". There are only four: operations, marketing, finance, HR. Employees, Customers and Outsourcing aren't functions — Finance was the only possible answer

Q5 — double-highlighted

Correct is (B) Volume. "Produces standardised steel bars" = high volume, low variety. Variation in demand means demand fluctuating over a cycle, which the stem never describes. A one-off increase is not variation.

The three calculation traps

  • Q11 — GP ratio 35% → GP = $630,000. COGS = Sales − GP = $1,170,000. Option (B) is the GP, not the COGS.
  • Q15 — run the balance month by month: 10k → 20k → 10k → 5k → $20,000. Don't net the totals.
  • Q20 — market share, not viewer count. P3 goes 21.43% → 24.14% = +2.71pp. P4 held 400,000 viewers but lost share.

⚠️ Two hard lessons from the marked script

1. "Identify TWO" means two distinct reasons. Reason (i) was crossed out entirely in 23(a). "Cost saving" and "time saving" are effectively the same reason stated twice, and the induction-training claim is wrong — the outsourcing provider still runs induction. Pick reasons from genuinely different categories:

Valid distinct reasons for outsourcing HR
Cost reduction — avoids maintaining an in-house HR department
Access to specialist expertise — external providers hold current legal and industrial relations knowledge
Allows focus on core business — management attention redirected to revenue-generating activity
Scalability — capacity flexes with business need without hiring

2. End on a tangible outcome, not a syllabus category. The 21(c) model closed on "the performance objectives of quality, dependability and cost are met." That is abstract. The teacher wants the business consequence:

…ensuring fewer defective products reach customers, which reduces warranty replacement costs and restores customer satisfaction.

Applies to every Operations answer. Name the concrete benefit, then the syllabus term — not the term alone.

The three patterns costing marks

  1. Not using the data given. The industry averages in Q24 were printed directly above the question and went uncited. If a figure is in the stimulus, quote it back.
  2. Warm-up sentences. "X plays a distinct role in…", "One key factor that can influence…", "Financial reports can be complicated…" — roughly 15% of the writing restated the question. Start at the definition.
  3. Answering beyond the question. A third of 23(c) covered a recession when the question asked about an increase in economic activity. Nothing outside the verb's scope earns anything.

Length and time budget

Marks Words Time
2 35–45 3 min
4 90–110 6 min
6 140–160 9 min

Anything past this isn't earning. It's costing you Section III.

3. Model Answers — Short Answer

21(a) · Outline how measuring warranty claims assists assessment of operations effectiveness · 2 marks

Warranty claims are a key performance indicator of quality. Measuring them against a target allows TWG to compare actual to planned performance, locate where the transformation process is producing defects, and take corrective action.

38 words. The two words that pay are KPI and quality.

⚠️ Terminology trap: a bottleneck is a capacity constraint slowing throughput, not a defect. Warranty claims reveal defects.

21(c) · Explain why controls are important in the operations process · 4 marks

⚠️ This scored 3/4 as originally written. The corrected final sentence is below.

Controls in the operations process involve comparing actual performance against planned performance using key performance indicators such as defect rates, lead times and warranty claims, then investigating the variance and taking corrective action. Controls are important because they convert monitoring into improvement: measurement alone identifies a problem, but control resolves it. For TWG, if warranty claims are targeted at 2% but reach 7%, control would trigger an investigation of the transformation process, potentially revealing inadequate quality assurance at the assembly stage. Controls also prevent minor variances escalating into systemic failure, as detecting a defect during production costs substantially less than replacing products under warranty. Consequently, controls ensure fewer defective appliances reach customers, reducing warranty replacement costs and restoring the customer satisfaction TWG is currently losing — achieving the performance objectives of quality and dependability, and enabling product differentiation.

Original ending (3/4) Corrected ending (4/4)
Closes on "the performance objectives of quality, dependability and cost are met" "fewer defective appliances reach customers, reducing warranty replacement costs and restoring customer satisfaction"
Why Names the syllabus category Names the business outcome, then the category

The words markers look for: variance and corrective action. Inventing a benchmark (2% vs 7%) shows you understand control needs something to compare against. The mark most people drop: ending on abstraction. Always finish with what actually changes for the business — customers retained, costs reduced, orders delivered on time.

22(a) · Outline ONE factor influencing consumer choice · 2 marks

Sociocultural influences, particularly reference groups such as peers and fitness influencers. Activewear is worn publicly, so women buy the brands their peers and influencers endorse, treating the product as a marker of social identity.

35 words.

The trap: influences on consumer choice = why the customer buys. Branding, packaging, price = what the business does. Don't cross the two.

The four factors:

Factor Meaning
Psychological Inside the individual — perception, motives, attitudes, personality, self-image, learning
Sociocultural Other people — family, peers and reference groups, social class, culture and subculture
Economic Boom vs recession, disposable income, level of economic activity
Government Legislation, taxes, interest rates, regulations affecting spending

22(b) · Explain how consumer laws influence marketing practices · 4 marks

The five consumer laws — memorise as a list:

deceptive and misleading advertising
price discrimination
implied conditions
warranties
resale price maintenance

Correct citation: Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth), containing the Australian Consumer Law (ACL), enforced by the ACCC.

Application example: Nellie and Ellie could not pin or clip garments on models to alter the fit shown, as this constitutes deceptive and misleading conduct by misrepresenting how the product performs.

22(c) · How can this business conduct market research · 4 marks

The question says conduct, so the process is the spine:

1. Determining information needs
2. Data collection    (primary / secondary)
3. Data analysis and interpretation

Primary and secondary alone is step 2 only. Add step 1 (what do they need to know — price sensitivity, preferred fabrics, purchase frequency?) and step 3, and name market segmentation by demographic and psychographic.

23(c) · Explain how an increase in economic activity may impact HR · 4 marks

Boom only. Say nothing about recession.

Low unemployment → skills shortages → upward wage pressure → harder to attract and retain staff → HR shifts spending toward retention strategies, training and non-financial rewards, while labour costs rise as a proportion of expenses.

24(b) · Discuss the profitability of Wacky Widgets · 4 marks

Wacky Widgets' net profit ratio is 24.2% ($206,000 ÷ $850,000) — 24 cents retained per sales dollar, well below the industry average of 50%. However, its gross profit ratio of 82.9% shows cost of goods sold is well controlled, so the weakness lies in operating expenses. This is confirmed by the expense ratio of 58.7%, far above the industry average of 36%, with wages alone absorbing $310,000. Profitability is therefore weak but correctable. Expense minimisation through cost centre analysis, and refinancing the $110,000 in loan repayments, would lift the net profit ratio toward the benchmark.

95 words.

The three ratios read against each other:

Ratio Wacky Widgets Industry
Gross profit 82.9% —
Net profit 24.2% 50%
Expense 58.7% 36%

The move that lifts this from 2 to 4: high GP + low NP means the money is leaking between them, i.e. operating expenses. Part (a) already proved it — reuse the figure.

⚠️ A ratio without a comparison is a number, not analysis. Always answer: compared to what? Over time, industry, or target. And if the paper hands you an industry average, use it — that was the mark lost here.

24(c) · Why are capitalising expenses and normalised earnings limitations · 4 marks

Capitalising expenses records a cost as part of a non-current asset's value rather than as an expense, writing it off as depreciation over time. This understates expenses and overstates both net profit and asset values, so the business appears stronger than it is. Normalised earnings are profits adjusted to remove one-off items such as an asset sale. Because management decides what counts as unusual, the adjustment is subjective, earnings can be smoothed, and comparability between years and between businesses is lost. Both therefore prevent financial reports giving a true and fair view.

92 words.

What it is Why it's a limitation
Capitalising expenses Adding a cost to the value of a non-current asset instead of expensing it now Understates expenses, overstates profit and asset values
Normalised earnings Adjusting profit to remove one-off or cyclical items Management decides what is "unusual" — subjective, destroys comparability

The hook: Capitalising expenses flatters the numbers. Normalised earnings flatter the story. Both are limitations because management chooses.

⚠️ Capitalised costs are written off as depreciation, not "as income."

4. Section III — Model Business Report

Question 25 (20 marks). Jumbo Movies Pty Ltd, cinema complex, Western Sydney. Decline in under-30 attendance, growing demand for streaming. New owners want profits up 20% in 12 months.

Review revealed: wet and slippery floors · run down environment and uniforms · broken coffee machine took 6 months to replace · regular ticketing machine errors · employees working through lunch breaks · dissatisfaction with training on new machines.

Required: describe the role of operations management · demonstrate how legal regulation and CSR influence operations · recommend TWO HR strategies.

✅ Teacher-marked 16–17/20. Margin note beside the legal and CSR section: "great paragraphs." Marks lost for combining the two HR strategies into one section. Teacher: "Do as 2 separate paragraphs, 2 reasons for each" · "link to HR" · "if you did the HR strategies separately you would get 18/20." The version below is corrected. ~780 words. If short on time, trim the CSR social examples rather than the HR section.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jumbo Movies Pty Ltd operates a cinema complex in a large shopping centre in Western Sydney, competing in an entertainment market disrupted by streaming services and declining attendance among consumers under 30. This report describes the role of operations management for the business, demonstrates how legal regulation and corporate social responsibility influence that function, and recommends two human resource strategies, being training and development as well as rewards, to assist the new owners in achieving their objective of increasing profits by 20% within 12 months.

THE ROLE OF OPERATIONS MANAGEMENT

Operations management involves the transformation of inputs into outputs, and for Jumbo Movies the output is intangible and perishable, as an unsold seat for a session cannot be recovered. The strategic role of operations is cost leadership or good and service differentiation, and Jumbo Movies cannot compete with streaming on cost, as Netflix carries no venue or staffing overheads, so it must pursue service differentiation by delivering an experience that cannot be replicated at home. Cinema operations are also high visibility, as customers are physically present and directly observe the facilities, staff and uniforms, which is why the run down environment identified in the review damages this business far more than it would a manufacturer. The review further reveals failures in the performance objectives of dependability, as a broken coffee machine took six months to replace, and quality, evidenced by regular ticketing machine errors. Operations is therefore interdependent with finance, as the 20% profit objective requires both cost control and higher revenue per customer, and with marketing, as an outdated environment contradicts any promotional message about a premium experience.

INFLUENCE OF LEGAL REGULATION AND CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY

Under the Work Health and Safety Act, Jumbo Movies owes a duty of care to employees and customers, and floors that are often wet and slippery constitute a direct breach, exposing the business to workers compensation claims, public liability claims and regulatory penalties. Operations must therefore build compliance into its processes through scheduled cleaning between sessions, hazard signage and staff safety training, while the Fair Work Act and National Employment Standards require that employees receive their entitled breaks, meaning rostering must be redesigned. Although compliance raises operating costs, a single serious injury claim would cost far more. Corporate social responsibility requires the business to move beyond this legal minimum, considering its impact on people, the community and the environment, and is voluntary and reputational rather than mandatory. As a cinema consumes substantial electricity through projection and air conditioning, Jumbo Movies could install laser projectors and LED lighting, eliminate single use plastics at the candy bar and introduce recycling. Although these initiatives raise short term costs and create tension with the profit objective, they support the triple bottom line and differentiate the business to consumers under 30, the very segment being lost to streaming.

RECOMMENDATION ONE — TRAINING AND DEVELOPMENT

It is recommended that Jumbo Movies implement a structured training and development program, a human resource strategy within the development stage of the HR cycle. The first reason is that it directly addresses the errors identified in the review. Employees are dissatisfied with the training received on the new ticketing machines and regular errors are occurring, indicating the technology was introduced without adequate skill development, so on the job training combined with customer service training would reduce these errors and improve the performance objectives of quality and speed. The second reason is retention. Employees who are supported to develop are more motivated and less likely to leave, reducing staff turnover, a key indicator of human resource effectiveness, which lowers recruitment and induction costs and contributes directly to the 20% profit objective.

RECOMMENDATION TWO — REWARDS

It is secondly recommended that Jumbo Movies introduce a combination of monetary and non monetary rewards, a maintenance strategy within the HR cycle. The first reason is that the review identifies clear sources of dissatisfaction that cost almost nothing to resolve. Guaranteed rostered lunch breaks, flexible rostering around peak demand, new uniforms and formal staff recognition address the complaints raised at minimal expense. The second reason is alignment. Monetary rewards linked to performance, such as a bonus tied to confectionery revenue per customer, align employee behaviour with the owners' profit objective. Together these reduce absenteeism and staff turnover, and produce the motivated, well presented workforce on which the cinema's service differentiation depends.

CONCLUSION

Service differentiation is Jumbo Movies only viable response to streaming competition. Legal regulation imposes safety and employment obligations, while corporate social responsibility differentiates it to a younger market. Training and development combined with a rewards system would improve service quality, morale and productivity, supporting the 20% profit objective within 12 months.

Every stimulus detail is used

Review point Where it lands
Wet, slippery floors WHS duty of care breach
Run down environment and uniforms High visibility (4 Vs) + non-monetary rewards
Coffee machine, 6 months Dependability failure
Ticketing machine errors Quality failure + training
Working through lunch breaks Fair Work / NES + non-monetary rewards
Unhappy with training Training and development

If short on time, cut in this order: the CSR social examples, then the variation-in-demand sentence, then the performance-pay risk sentence. Takes you to ~850 without losing a criterion.

5. Section III Report Template

The structure that earns the "form of a business report" criterion:

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY        who the business is, what the report covers,
                         name the recommendations up front

BODY SECTION 1           one heading per bullet in the question
BODY SECTION 2           judgement-first topic sentence
                         stimulus detail as evidence
                         "Therefore..." closer

RECOMMENDATION ONE       ← ONE HEADING PER RECOMMENDATION
RECOMMENDATION TWO         never combine them

CONCLUSION               restate findings + the recommendations + the objective

⚠️ The rule that cost marks here: if the question says "Recommend TWO strategies", that is two separate headed paragraphs, each with two reasons, each explicitly named as a strategy of the relevant business function. Combining them reads as one recommendation and caps the mark.

Each recommendation paragraph:

1  NAME       It is recommended that [BUSINESS] implement [STRATEGY],
              a [FUNCTION] strategy within [SYLLABUS CATEGORY].
2  REASON 1   The first reason is [X]. [STIMULUS EVIDENCE].
3  REASON 2   The second reason is [Y]. [EFFECT + INDICATOR].
4  LINK       …contributing directly to [THE STATED OBJECTIVE].

Word budget: 750–800 words, ~35 minutes. Roughly 20 words a minute handwritten.
